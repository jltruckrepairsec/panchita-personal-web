# Succession

> **Status:** concept — named in the Master Blueprint. No definition given.
> **Lifecycle:** CONCEPT ONLY
> **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-10

## What is known

Approved as a governance/long-horizon concept, and named directly in
[P14](../00-master-blueprint/principles.md#p14--succession-and-governance).

## Succession and Legacy

[Legacy](legacy.md) is the *goal*: Panchita survives changes of owners,
employees, vendors, models and infrastructure. Succession is plausibly the
*mechanism* — what actually happens when one of those changes occurs.

That reading is inference. Both names are approved and neither is defined, so
they are kept as separate pages rather than merged by guess.

## The uncomfortable current state

Succession is the concept the system is furthest from, and it is worth stating
plainly rather than softly:

* **One person can operate Panchita.** No runbook exists for anyone else.
* **The credentials are unmapped.** Nothing records what exists, where, or who
  could reach it.
* **The intent is unwritten.** [00 · Vision](../00-master-blueprint/vision.md)
  is empty, so a successor would inherit a system with no statement of what it
  is for.
* **The procedures are unwritten.** [05 · SOPs](../05-knowledge/sops.md) is
  empty.

None of that is a criticism of a system this young. It is the honest baseline
that any succession work would start from.

## The cheapest thing that would help

Not a succession plan — an **inventory**: what systems exist, what each costs,
who holds the credentials, and what breaks if each disappears. That is mostly
already wanted for other reasons
([08 · Provider Costs](../08-cost-and-quality/provider-costs.md),
[04 · Audit](../04-security/audit.md)), and it is the artefact a successor would
need on day one.

Worth knowing; **not scheduled** — see
[00 · Execution Priority](../00-master-blueprint/execution-priority.md).

## Deliberately undefined

What triggers succession, who succeeds, what they receive, and whether this is a
technical, operational or legal concern.
